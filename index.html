“Plans are nothing; planning is everything.”— Dwight D. Eisenhower

He was not saying that plans are useless. He was making a distinction between having a plan and being prepared.

A simple example

Imagine planning your retirement.

The plan:
Withdraw a specific amount from your investments every month.
Reality:
The stock market falls sharply, or an unexpected medical expense occurs.
The value of planning:
Because you considered those possibilities beforehand, you have alternatives. You might temporarily reduce withdrawals, use cash reserves, or adjust your spending.

The plan changed, but the planning helped you respond intelligently. That is what testing “What If” scenarios is for.

Your average monthly spending. Easiest way: total all money going out (withdrawals, checks, card payments from banks and payments to 3rd parties from brokerage accounts) on 12 months from bank statements/brokerage statements, then divide by 12. Leave out large one-time costs that likely won’t reoccur again during retirement — e.g., a car bought with cash, or a home down payment, air conditioner/costly appliances, etc.

At retirement start, in today's dollars

Today's value of your savings and investments, i.e. brokerage accounts — interest, dividends, capital appreciation — IRAs, 401(k)s, CDs, savings... income-producing assets! Don’t include your home, cars, or personal belongings.

Total value today

How much you add to your invested assets each month until you retire — 401(k) contributions, IRA deposits, regular savings. Assets at retirement = current invested assets + (monthly addition × # of months until retirement). Enter 0 if none or already retired. Calculator takes care of the math, all needed here is current invested assets!
